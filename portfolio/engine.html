ERCOT CRR Engine

Texas's grid sells the right to collect congestion rent between two points: a Congestion Revenue Right. Pricing one means predicting, months ahead, how weather, plant and line outages, fuel, demand and the network itself will price every node. This engine does that for every path in every auction. It rebuilds itself every night from primary market data, admits a covariate only after it survives statistical gates, stages catastrophes as scenarios, grades its own predictions against settlement, and never places a trade: the human selects, sizes and submits.

I am its sole architect. Claude Fable 5 began the build with me on 2026-07-02 (the first ten commits), and the record of its 320 commits names a co-author on 290: Fable 5 on 153, Opus on 137.

What it has to get right

Every variable that moves a congestion price

Congestion is an outcome of the whole system, so the engine models it from every side and composes them in one coordinate system: nodes and binding-constraint families, hourly. Each group below is a set of pipeline stages with its own feeds, validation and tests.

The network

  • 1,295 settlement points: 1,145 resource nodes, hubs and load zones, each tracked through its lifecycle (1,052 online, 78 newborn, 61 gone dark)
  • Binding-constraint families and shift factors; the path value is composed from them, never fitted per pair
  • Contingencies, stability limits (GTCs) and the transmission outage register
  • ERCOT's CRR network model, read each auction

Supply and outages

  • 992 plants with heat rates and marginal costs: a merit order by region (below)
  • Dispatch forecast on weather-driven net load, and a scarcity adder
  • Nuclear status daily from the NRC; quiet derates read from SCED disclosures; retirements; coal stocks; gas basis
  • 837 interconnection-queue projects (207 GW) attributed to the nodes they will create

Demand and behavior

  • Load forecast, seven days, by region
  • Measured load response to temperature, by region and month (the U-curve, below)
  • Regional load-share regimes

Weather, macro to micro

  • Months ahead: climate indices condition the tails of the auction months
  • Weeks ahead: a sub-seasonal outbreak challenger
  • Days ahead: the live load forecast triggers; storms enter as two knobs, a transmission derate and a demand-destruction haircut

The auction itself

  • Clearing model; fill curves from 4.85M historical bids (bids above clearing fill 95.7%, bids at the margin 32.7%)
  • Winner's-curse haircut: you clear exactly when the crowd valued the path lower
  • Option put-call parity, which the auction enforces exactly, kept as a data-corruption tripwire
  • Clearing-price regime tagged by era, before and after ERCOT's real-time co-optimization

Who else is trading

  • Every holder's positions, resales and P&L, tied to settlement
  • Informed positioning, bid-time knowledge, follow flags
  • Read-only and quarantined from pricing by architecture: the edge layer can be deleted without breaking a price

The engine rebuilt the market's ledger from ERCOT's primary auction files and day-ahead prices, corrected the math, and now reconciles to ERCOT's own settlement report (Portfolio-Adhoc). The weather inversion

A hurricane made Houston cheap

The textbook trade says extreme weather raises demand and prices into the damage. Beryl, July 2024, did the opposite. It destroyed distribution into Houston, 2.7 million customers went dark, load collapsed, and congestion into Houston inverted. ERCOT's settlement records the result.

The engine's storm knob had one sign for every storm, "hurricane = congestion up". That describes a transmission-loss storm: load intact, imports constrained. Beryl was a demand-destruction storm. The fix gives every event two independent knobs. A transmission and generation derate raises import congestion. A load haircut for distribution damage and evacuation lowers the zone and raises counterflow value. The storm slider drives both, anchored on Beryl: a category-1 landfall takes about 42% off the region's load, capped at 50%, and an offshore storm counts as zero.

"Weather never prices anything directly. BEHAVIOR does." Any new weather feature must name the behavior it claims and cite a measured response, or it doesn't ship.

Beryl is one mechanism of several. The sections below follow a weather signal from the climate drivers months ahead down to the node, and then stage every kind of catastrophe the engine has catalogued.

Same cold, different month

Measured load response, by region and month. In cold weeks at least 8 °F below the month's norm, North Texas load rose about 5,432 MW in January and about 10 MW in November, roughly 540 times less for the same anomaly. A freeze at 100% in November is a contract-tested no-op. A storm near Houston with no landfall (Francine, September 2024) moved load −0.2%: no response, no trade.

A read-only screen watches for regional demand shocks of this kind and never moves the core forecast; its trigger and statistics stay off this page. The rigorous version is still open, by the engine's own backlog: re-solving the constraint space with storm outages as contingencies.

Testing covariates: a scan built to fail

"Regress the whole universe, I don't want to take anyone's word for it." The macro scan crossed eight climate drivers (ONI, Niño 3.4, SOI, the Arctic Oscillation, NAO, PNA, PDO, AMO) with four regions, twelve target months, the mean and the variance of temperature, and leads of two to four months, over 75 years: 2,304 tests. Chance alone predicts about 115 false positives at p < .05; 362 came in. 57 survived false-discovery control, and 48 also held the same sign with |r| ≥ 0.2 in both halves of the record, against about 3 expected under the null. The hindcast gate

A conditioned forecast has to beat the flat climatological one, by Brier score, over 74-75 years of hindcasts, region by region and month by month. 21 of 48 cells win. Trust in a cell takes both: surviving false-discovery control and winning its hindcast. The macro lane sizes tail exposure, never the core value.

Lifting covariates into the forecasters

Day-ahead basis forecasters take 127 as-of covariates, each stamped with when it could first have been known: ten years of daily weather by region, forecast temperatures for days one to seven, gas at Henry Hub, Waha and Houston Ship Channel, new outage megawatts, nuclear availability, load, system wind and solar, binding-constraint counts, heat streaks. Promotion rules: evidence promotes, humans check

The founding doctrine made execution human-only. In July 2026 I ratified its scope. It governs bid submission, and nothing ever auto-submits to ERCOT. It is not a license for human approval queues over the evidence. Promotion authority belongs to the evidence gate, because "humans are not good Bayesians" (Kahneman and Tversky). A trader who just lived through Beryl will over-rotate to hurricanes, and a human approval queue becomes a bias-injection point and a staleness bottleneck. Autonomy is tiered by how much the evidence can carry:

  • Levers move only on every month. A challenger setting must beat the incumbent's rank correlation by at least 0.005 on every one of the 14 replayed auctions, within fixed bounds, with the receipt written down.
  • A governor watches the streams. 24 model streams under a CUSUM detector: 14 in regime break, 2 outliers, 7 stable. Outliers go to the tail layer, never the central weights. A shift sustained three observations opens a decision card (22 open), and hand-set numbers older than three months are flagged for aging.
  • Every gate turn is ledgered. 5,489 promotion and demotion events, append-only; 107 gates on tonight.
  • Screens are not signals. Discovery screens rank on probability (Beta-Binomial, shrunk, with intervals), not expected value, so one Uri month cannot make a path look reliable. No screen reaches the Bid Desk until its walk-forward replay gate runs.
  • Human conjectures are labeled, not trusted. A knob a person sets enters only signed, tagged as user-sourced, and bannered in red wherever it rides.

The climatological layer: probability, duration, and the tail it moves

Weather enters the price only as a scenario mixture: a named event with a probability, a duration and a severity. Each probability carries its climatological source on the record. Freeze: four stations statewide at or below 22 °F for 48 hours, 1955-2026, December to February pooled 23 of 215. Hurricane: Texas tropical-storm-or-stronger landfalls by month since 1950. Heat dome: Dallas 10-day runs at or above 100 °F. The multipliers are hand-set, and the artifact says so ("hand-set and unvalidated"). Scenarios move the 95th percentile, which is what sizes a bid, more than the mean: January 2027's freeze probability of 19.7% lifts the mean severity ×1.197 but the tail ×1.394, across 1,101 of 4,455 constraint families.

Solvers, pipelines and invariants

S06 · graph-Laplacian geolocation

Placing 923 settlement points on the map with one sparse solve

The problem. When the build started, only about 288 of some 1,200 settlement points had coordinates, yet every one has a price and belongs on the map. Transmission branches are geographically short, so a bus sits close to the mean of its electrical neighbours. That makes the unknown positions a Dirichlet boundary problem on the network graph, with buses matched to known coordinates as the boundary.

minxI∑(i,j)∈E(xi−xj)2subject toxk=x^k∀k∈BL=D−WLIIxI=−LIBxB
  1. Restrict to the largest connected component: islands make L singular.
  2. Build the unweighted adjacency W and degree D as sparse matrices; L = D − W.
  3. Partition into interior I and anchors B; solve x_I = spsolve(L_II, −L_IB · x_B), once for latitude, once for longitude.
  4. Tag every estimate and never let it overwrite a real coordinate (published point > asset centroid > estimate).
A = csc_matrix((vals, (rows, cols)), shape=(n, n))
L = csc_matrix((deg, (range(n), range(n))), shape=(n, n)) - A

interior = ~anchored & (deg > 0)
Lii = L[interior][:, interior].tocsc()
Lib = L[interior][:, anchored]

def solve(anchor_vals: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    x = np.full(n, np.nan)
    x[anchored] = anchor_vals[anchored]
    rhs = -Lib @ anchor_vals[anchored]
    x[interior] = spsolve(Lii, rhs)
    return x

lat = solve(anchor_lat)
lon = solve(anchor_lon)

S07d · DC power flow and shift factors

Exact shift factors from the auction's network model, and a sign guard

The engine. It parses the auction network model (PSS/E v30 RAW), keeps in-service branches with |x| > 10⁻⁷, takes the largest connected component and drops the slack bus. It builds the DC susceptance matrix, factors it once with a sparse LU, and then runs one triangular solve per monitored element.

Bii′=∑l∋i1xl,Bij′=−1xl(B′)Ty=ea−ebxm,ISFm,k=ykSFP→Q=ISFQ−ISFP

For each in-service branch l = (i, j) with reactance xl; for each monitored element m = (a, b); the shift factor of a path from P to Q is sink minus source.

The sanity guard. RAW branch orientation is arbitrary relative to the market's monitored direction, so a family's sign is a labeling convention, not physics. Against published shift factors, 15 of 31 constraint families came out reversed. Each family's sign is aligned to the published data (flip where the per-family correlation is negative), and the magnitude is the honest physics check: per-family |corr| median 0.90 (interquartile 0.75 to 0.99) over 5,802 overlapping node and constraint pairs. The exact factors replace the snapshot ones as a versioned swap; the interface does not change.

Kept on the machine. The network model is ECEII under ERCOT Protocol 1.3.2. Model files and per-bus values never leave the machine; only derived analytics do.

S12b · a market invariant as a data guard

Put-call parity, enforced by the clearing LP to the cent

The identity. An obligation one way plus an option the other way pays the option, hour by hour, for any spread s:

OBLP→Q+OPTQ→Ppayss+max(0,−s)=max(0,s)=OPTP→Qedge=πOPT,P→Q−(πOBL,P→Q+πOPT,Q→P)=0

Within one auction the clearing LP prices all three legs jointly, so no-arbitrage forces the edge to zero. In May 2026 all 7,214 pairs cleared at 0.000. For example, HB_SOUTH → HB_WEST: obligation −4.952 plus reverse option 10.747 equals 5.795, the direct option. Tonight's Oct 2026 auction: 7,851 pairs, largest edge 0.000.

How it grades itself

The same rubric discipline, applied to a model of a market

The engine ranks its own evidence the way the graders in this package rank theirs, and it labels every number with the rung it earned. A replay rebuilt with today's pools is a diagnostic, and the label travels with the number.

  1. 1External oracle. Realized day-ahead settlement and ERCOT's settlement reports.
  2. 2Falsification. Walk-forward tests a component can fail, against the incumbent and seasonal-naive.
  3. 3Invariants. Identities that must hold: put-call parity, the sign of the congestion identity, the calendar.
  4. 4Same-author re-derivation. In the engine's words, "tautological — not verification".

Gates that promote, humans who check

  • Walk-forward skill. A forecaster is trusted only if it beats seasonal-naive by 10% on holdout. Building that gate caught a baseline bug that had inflated skills to 0.9.
  • False-discovery control. A 75-year climate scan ran 2,304 tests under FDR control; 48 survivors were stable out of sample, against about 3 expected by chance.
  • A human signs the thin ones. One macro signal was one of 6 stable survivors out of 1,416 tests, about 3 expected by chance. It wins its hindcast (Brier 0.228 vs 0.242 flat, walk-forward, 45 years). It ships only because I signed it (2026-07-26). It abstains whenever its driver is not yet published, and it sizes tail exposure only, never the core value.
  • Refuted, and kept on record. A forward monthly day-ahead price model lost to persistence, out of sample, so it stays advisory.

Built with AI agents, governed like a market desk

Seven contracts, and a rule for who decides

  • Claude Fable 5 153
  • Claude Opus 4.8 120
  • Claude Opus 5.5 17
  • no co-author trailer 30

Commits per day by co-author trailer: 303 in July 2026, when the engine was built, then 17 in October. In between it ran every night on its own.

The founding specification binds seven contracts. Several exist because an earlier agent silently broke what they protect:

  1. Constraint-space doctrine. Model nodes and constraints, never source-sink pairs.
  2. Option valuation. Options pay the hourly positive part; option risk is the premium paid. (An earlier agent valued options off monthly means after being told otherwise.)
  3. Clearing-price regime. No structural auction discount assumed; every anchor carries an era tag.
  4. Forecasting-model boundary. A challenger never defines the baseline, never moves clearing cost, never overwrites a value.
  5. Awardable-MW non-filter. Informational columns never filter, rank or drop a candidate.
  6. Weather scenarios. Weather enters only as a scenario mixture with duration and probability.
  7. Residuals. One-offs become scenarios, never weights: "Uri is a state with a probability, not a February tilt".

Evidence promotes, humans check. Statistical gates (walk-forward skill, false-discovery control, oracle reconciliation, contract tests) promote automatically. Humans hold veto, one-click revert and sole execution authority. Human conjectures enter only signed, labeled and bannered in red. Every agent session reads a dated decision log before changing behavior, and a completeness matrix allows no silent "done".

Bugs caught by validating, now permanent guards

  • The leading minus sign of the congestion identity was dropped, inverting every path. A contingency was multi-counted. The first replay against realized settlement caught both.
  • Month names sort alphabetically: max(auction_name) pinned the whole engine to May. Artifacts are now chosen by date.
  • ERCOT's archive prices carry a leading space. An untrimmed cast made them null, and greatest(0, NULL) = 0 silently zeroed option payouts. Ingest now strips, and refuses partitions over 1% null.
  • A missing day inside a partition reported "32/32 COMPLETE" for four months. Completeness is now checked by day, nightly.
  • A stale 0.87 sat on the desk while the artifact said 0.75. Desk numbers are now read live, never copied.
