ERCOT CRR Engine

Texas's grid sells the right to collect congestion rent between two points: a Congestion Revenue Right. Pricing one means predicting, months ahead, how weather, plant and line outages, fuel, demand and the network itself will price every node. This engine does that for every path in every auction. It rebuilds itself every night from primary market data, admits a covariate only after it survives statistical gates, stages catastrophes as scenarios, grades its own predictions against settlement, and never places a trade: the human selects, sizes and submits.

I am its sole architect. The build began on 2 July 2026 with Claude Code coding agents.

What it has to get right

Every variable that moves a congestion price

Congestion is an outcome of the whole system, so the engine models it from every side and composes them in one coordinate system: nodes and binding-constraint families, hourly. Each group below is a set of pipeline stages with its own feeds, validation and tests.

The network

  • 1,295 settlement points: 1,145 resource nodes, hubs and load zones, each tracked through its lifecycle (1,052 online, 78 newborn, 61 gone dark)
  • Binding-constraint families and shift factors; the path value is composed from them, never fitted per pair
  • Contingencies, stability limits (GTCs) and the transmission outage register, captured hourly into an append-only history
  • ERCOT's CRR network model, solved exactly for every forward month and gated against ERCOT's published auction prices (below)

Supply and outages

  • 997 plants with heat rates and marginal costs: a merit order by region (below)
  • Dispatch forecast on weather-driven net load, and a scarcity adder
  • Nuclear status daily from the NRC; quiet derates read from SCED disclosures; retirements; coal stocks; gas basis
  • 837 interconnection-queue projects (207 GW) attributed to the nodes they will create

Demand and behavior

  • Load forecast, seven days, by region
  • Measured load response to temperature, by region and month (the U-curve, below)
  • Regional load-share regimes
  • Hidden load: load the history cannot see (data centers, crypto mining), measured from three registers and forecast with P10–P90 bands; ERCOT's Batch Zero large-load cohorts placed by month and zone

Weather, macro to micro

  • Months ahead: climate indices condition the tails of the auction months
  • Weeks ahead: a sub-seasonal outbreak challenger
  • Days 1 to 14: a 64-member ensemble (WeatherNext 3) as the forecast start point
  • Days ahead: the live load forecast triggers; storms enter as two knobs, a transmission derate and a demand-destruction haircut

The auction itself

  • Clearing model; fill curves from 4.85M historical bids (bids above clearing fill 95.7%, bids at the margin 32.7%)
  • P(clear) curves shrunk from each path's own history toward its region-pair family, and the bid that maximizes expected cleared profit
  • Winner's-curse haircut: you clear exactly when the crowd valued the path lower
  • Settlement deration caps at resource nodes; wind and solar capped at zero upside
  • Option put-call parity, which the auction enforces exactly, kept as a data-corruption tripwire
  • Clearing-price regime tagged by era, before and after ERCOT's real-time co-optimization

Who else is trading

  • Every holder's positions, resales and P&L, tied to settlement
  • Informed positioning, bid-time knowledge, follow flags
  • Read-only and quarantined from pricing by architecture: the edge layer can be deleted without breaking a price

Settlement truth

The vendor got it wrong. The engine corrected it and tied out to actual.

ERCOT is the oracle. Before the engine had API access to ERCOT's settlement data, it worked from a commercial SaaS extract, and the transactions didn't tie. The engine rebuilt the market's ledger from ERCOT's primary auction files and day-ahead prices, corrected the math, and now reconciles to ERCOT's own settlement report (Portfolio-Adhoc). That reconciliation is now two permanent contract tests, and both run in the nightly suite.

The weather inversion

A hurricane made Houston cheap

The textbook trade says extreme weather raises demand and prices into the damage. Beryl, July 2024, did the opposite. It destroyed distribution into Houston, 2.7 million customers went dark, load collapsed, and congestion into Houston inverted. ERCOT's settlement records the result.

The engine's storm knob had one sign for every storm, "hurricane = congestion up". That describes a transmission-loss storm: load intact, imports constrained. Beryl was a demand-destruction storm. The fix gives every event two independent knobs. A transmission and generation derate raises import congestion. A load haircut for distribution damage and evacuation lowers the zone and raises counterflow value. The storm slider drives both, anchored on Beryl: a category-1 landfall takes about 42% off the region's load, capped at 50%, and an offshore storm counts as zero.

"Weather never prices anything directly. BEHAVIOR does." Any new weather feature must name the behavior it claims and cite a measured response, or it doesn't ship.

Beryl is one mechanism of several. The sections below follow a weather signal from the climate drivers months ahead down to the node, and then stage every kind of catastrophe the engine has catalogued.

Same cold, different month

Measured load response, by region and month. In cold weeks at least 8 °F below the month's norm, North Texas load rose about 5,432 MW in January and about 10 MW in November, roughly 540 times less for the same anomaly. A freeze at 100% in November is a contract-tested no-op. A storm near Houston with no landfall (Francine, September 2024) moved load −0.2%: no response, no trade.

A read-only screen watches for regional demand shocks of this kind and never moves the core forecast; its trigger and statistics stay off this page. The rigorous version is still open, by the engine's own backlog: re-solving the constraint space with storm outages as contingencies.

Testing covariates: a scan built to fail

"Regress the whole universe, I don't want to take anyone's word for it." The macro scan crossed eight climate drivers (ONI, Niño 3.4, SOI, the Arctic Oscillation, NAO, PNA, PDO, AMO) with four regions, twelve target months, the mean and the variance of temperature, and leads of two to four months, over 75 years: 2,304 tests. Chance alone predicts about 115 false positives at p < .05; 362 came in. 57 survived false-discovery control, and 48 also held the same sign with |r| ≥ 0.2 in both halves of the record, against about 3 expected under the null. The hindcast gate

A conditioned forecast has to beat the flat climatological one, by Brier score, over 74-75 years of hindcasts, region by region and month by month. 21 of 48 cells win. Trust in a cell takes both: surviving false-discovery control and winning its hindcast. The macro lane sizes tail exposure, never the core value.

Lifting covariates into the forecasters

Day-ahead basis forecasters take 130 as-of covariates, each stamped with when it could first have been known: ten years of daily weather by region, forecast temperatures for days one to seven, gas at Henry Hub, Waha and Houston Ship Channel, new outage megawatts, nuclear availability, load, system wind and solar, binding-constraint counts, heat streaks. A covariate set earns its place only through the gate: a forecaster is trusted on a corridor block only if it beats a seasonal-naive forecast by 10% on a three-fold, 14-day walk-forward. Promotion rules: evidence promotes, humans check

The founding doctrine made execution human-only. In July 2026 I ratified its scope. It governs bid submission, and nothing ever auto-submits to ERCOT. It is not a license for human approval queues over the evidence. Promotion authority belongs to the evidence gate, because "humans are not good Bayesians" (Kahneman and Tversky). A trader who just lived through Beryl will over-rotate to hurricanes, and a human approval queue becomes a bias-injection point and a staleness bottleneck. Autonomy is tiered by how much the evidence can carry:

TierWhat changesWho decides
1Bounded, reversible reweights (recency half-life)Automatic; a card notifies
2Component swaps under strong gates (walk-forward skill against the incumbent, FDR, oracle reconciliation, contract tests)Automatic after a visible veto window
3Thin-sample gates, or changes that rewrite history (era splits)Human check required. A small-sample gate has real false-positive risk: an outage signal once passed at 93× out of sample on a tiny sample, and the check caught it as an artifact
  • Levers move only on every month. A challenger setting must beat the incumbent's rank correlation by at least 0.005 on every one of the 14 replayed auctions, within fixed bounds, with the receipt written down.
  • A governor watches the streams. 24 model streams under a CUSUM detector (reference value 0.5σ, decision limit 4σ, detection from 8 observations). A spike of |z| ≥ 3 that reverts is an outlier and goes to the tail layer, never the central weights. A shift sustained 3 observations opens a decision card (23 open), and hand-set numbers older than 3 months are flagged for aging.
  • Every gate turn is ledgered. 6,193 promotion and demotion events, append-only; 111 gates hold trust.
  • Screens are not signals. Discovery screens rank on probability (Beta-Binomial, shrunk, with intervals), not expected value, so one Uri month cannot make a path look reliable. No screen reaches the Bid Desk until its walk-forward replay gate runs.
  • Human conjectures are labeled, not trusted. A knob a person sets enters only signed, tagged as user-sourced, and bannered in red wherever it rides.

The mechanisms, and why they predict opposite signs

Demand surgeHeat dome, freeze. Load rises through the measured response, the zone prices up, import congestion rises: paths that sink into the zone gain. A November freeze is a no-op.
Transport lossLines or plants out, load intact. Import capacity falls, so congestion into the zone rises: sink value up. The rigorous version re-solves the constraint space with the outage as a contingency.

The climatological layer: probability, duration, and the tail it moves

Weather enters the price only as a scenario mixture: a named event with a probability, a duration and a severity. Each probability carries its climatological source on the record. Freeze: four stations statewide at or below 22 °F for 48 hours, 1955-2026, December to February pooled 23 of 215. Hurricane: Texas tropical-storm-or-stronger landfalls by month since 1950. Heat dome: Dallas 10-day runs at or above 100 °F. The multipliers are hand-set, and the artifact says so ("hand-set and unvalidated"). Scenarios move the 95th percentile, which is what sizes a bid, more than the mean: January 2027's freeze probability of 19.7% lifts the mean severity ×1.197 but the tail ×1.394, across 1,067 of 4,474 constraint families.

Under the hood

Solvers, pipelines and invariants

Six pieces of the engine, as the code does them. The math is the code's math, and each receipt comes from the engine's own artifacts. The engine's source stays private, except for two short excerpts quoted verbatim: the Laplacian solve and the parity tripwire.

S06 · graph-Laplacian geolocation

Placing 923 settlement points on the map with one sparse solve

The problem. When the build started, only about 288 of some 1,200 settlement points had coordinates, yet every one has a price and belongs on the map. Transmission branches are geographically short, so a bus sits close to the mean of its electrical neighbours. That makes the unknown positions a Dirichlet boundary problem on the network graph, with buses matched to known coordinates as the boundary.

minxI∑(i,j)∈E(xi−xj)2subject toxk=x^k∀k∈BL=D−WLIIxI=−LIBxB
  1. Restrict to the largest connected component: islands make L singular.
  2. Build the unweighted adjacency W and degree D as sparse matrices; L = D − W.
  3. Partition into interior I and anchors B; solve x_I = spsolve(L_II, −L_IB · x_B), once for latitude, once for longitude.
  4. Tag every estimate and never let it overwrite a real coordinate (published point > asset centroid > estimate).
A = csc_matrix((vals, (rows, cols)), shape=(n, n))
L = csc_matrix((deg, (range(n), range(n))), shape=(n, n)) - A

interior = ~anchored & (deg > 0)
Lii = L[interior][:, interior].tocsc()
Lib = L[interior][:, anchored]

def solve(anchor_vals: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    x = np.full(n, np.nan)
    x[anchored] = anchor_vals[anchored]
    rhs = -Lib @ anchor_vals[anchored]
    x[interior] = spsolve(Lii, rhs)
    return x

lat = solve(anchor_lat)
lon = solve(anchor_lon)

S07h · exact shift factors, month by month

Every forward month solved on ERCOT's own auction model, and gated against ERCOT's published prices

The solve. Each delivery month is priced on the newest ERCOT CRR auction network model (PSS/E v30) that covers it. The engine keeps in-service elements with |x| > 10⁻⁷, solves the largest island, builds the DC susceptance matrix and factors it with a sparse LU. For every contingency a constraint family has bound under, it removes that contingency's devices, re-factorizes and solves again, so each family carries post-contingency sensitivities, weighted by shadow price over its contingencies.

Bii′=∑l∋i1xl,Bij′=−1xl(B′)Ty=ea−ebxm,ISFm,k=ykSFP→Q=ISFQ−ISFP

For each in-service branch l = (i, j) with reactance xl; for each monitored element m = (a, b); a settlement point's factor is its buses' factors weighted by participation; the shift factor of a path from P to Q is sink minus source. A contingency that splits the island is solved on the element's own component and flagged.

The oracle gate. With the solved factors and an auction's published binding shadow prices, the solver recomputes every settlement point's published auction price. A month may price nothing unless R² (slope held at 1, one offset per block) reaches 0.99 in every time-of-use block.

Orientation and confirmation. RAW branch direction is arbitrary relative to the market's monitored direction, so the sign convention is checked every build against ERCOT's posted day-ahead shift factors: the solved family vectors correlate with them at a median of 0.969 to 0.975 across 469 to 485 families in each of the 12 forward months, and a median at or below zero stops the build. Every family a month serves is then re-checked against ERCOT's newest 14 days of posted day-ahead and 7 days of real-time shift factors; a contradicted family is re-tiered and listed, never silently kept. Structure is gated too: every settlement point's participation factors sum to one (1,008 points, 0 violations), no output is non-finite, and every one of 1,967 families has a tier.

Kept on the machine. The network model is ECEII under ERCOT Protocol 1.3.2. Model files, element names and per-bus values never leave the machine; only derived analytics do.

S09 to S13 · the bid

A bid priced against a distribution of clearing prices

The auction is uniform-price. A buy bid is awarded at or above the clearing price C and pays C, not the bid. So the bid is solved against the distribution of C:

P(clear|b)=wFpath(b)+(1−w)Ffamily(b)w=npathnpath+6b*=argmaxbP(C≤b)·(V−E[C|C≤b])
  • Evidence-weighted shrinkage. A path's own clearing history is exact but thin; its region-pair family is coarse but deep. The weight moves to the path as its evidence grows, and post-RTC+B auctions count double.
  • Winner's curse, beside the bid. You clear exactly when the crowd valued the path lower. The haircut, E[V − realized | awarded], is measured on what a price-taker would have won, pooled by sink region, and rides as its own column: it never moves the bid.
  • Fills, measured. For a 1 MW bid after RTC+B: 0.81 of the MW fills at the margin, 0.90 within $0.25, 0.94 within $1 and 0.97 within $5.
  • Settlement deration. A payment into a resource node is capped at its resource-type limit; wind and solar nodes are capped at zero upside. Every one of 1,280 resource nodes carries a cap.
  • The basket. A binary program (cvxpy with HiGHS) marks an advisory basket under ERCOT's per-auction transaction limit and a per-sink cap, and publishes the near-misses. It decides nothing.
S08c · options

Options on the hourly positive part, re-centred exactly

An option pays max(0, sh) hour by hour, so its forward value is the expected positive part of the hourly spread distribution X, shifted by δ = v − E[X] to the month's own forecast level v:

OPThr=E[max(0,X+δ)]≥∑bwbmax(0,mb+δ)
  • First order: E[max(0, X)] + P(X > 0)·δ, clamped into the exact Jensen envelope. It drops the mass crossing the kink, so it is a strict under-estimate for every δ ≠ 0.
  • Exact grid: M equal-count strata with era-weighted means mb. It is mass-exact, the Jensen floor and the translation cap hold by construction, and it is a lower bound whose error is confined to one stratum and shrinks as 1/M.
  • A lever chooses the estimator, both are contract-tested, and every row says which one produced it.
S12b · a market invariant as a data guard

Put-call parity, enforced by the clearing LP to the cent

The identity. An obligation one way plus an option the other way pays the option, hour by hour, for any spread s:

OBLP→Q+OPTQ→Ppayss+max(0,−s)=max(0,s)=OPTP→Qedge=πOPT,P→Q−(πOBL,P→Q+πOPT,Q→P)=0

Within one auction the clearing LP prices all three legs jointly, so no-arbitrage forces the edge to zero. In May 2026 all 7,214 pairs cleared at 0.000. For example, HB_SOUTH → HB_WEST: obligation −4.952 plus reverse option 10.747 equals 5.795, the direct option. The Nov 2026 auction: 8,543 pairs, largest edge 0.000.

How it grades itself

The same rubric discipline, applied to a model of a market

The engine ranks its own evidence the way the graders in this package rank theirs, and it labels every number with the rung it earned. A replay rebuilt with today's pools is a diagnostic, and the label travels with the number.

  1. 1External oracle. Realized day-ahead settlement and ERCOT's settlement reports.
  2. 2Falsification. Walk-forward tests a component can fail, against the incumbent and seasonal-naive.
  3. 3Invariants. Identities that must hold: put-call parity, the sign of the congestion identity, the calendar.
  4. 4Same-author re-derivation. In the engine's words, "tautological — not verification".

Gates that promote, humans who check

  • Walk-forward skill. A forecaster is trusted only if it beats seasonal-naive by 10% on holdout. Building that gate caught a baseline bug that had inflated skills to 0.9.
  • False-discovery control. A 75-year climate scan ran 2,304 tests under FDR control; 48 survivors were stable out of sample, against about 3 expected by chance.
  • A human signs the thin ones. One macro signal was one of 6 stable survivors out of 1,416 tests, about 3 expected by chance. It wins its hindcast (Brier 0.228 vs 0.242 flat, walk-forward, 45 years). It ships only because I signed it (2026-07-26). It abstains whenever its driver is not yet published, and it sizes tail exposure only, never the core value.
  • Refuted, and kept on record. A forward monthly day-ahead price model lost to persistence, out of sample, so it stays advisory.
  • One scoreboard for the engines. The bid value, the four corridor challengers, climatology and the market's own clearing price are scored on one target, the month's realized day-ahead obligation value, at one- and two-month leads. A forecast counts only if the vintage archive shows its file written at or before the bid close.
  • Leakage measured, not asserted. As-of builds run on two clocks, auction close and settlement. The dark-value screen is rebuilt as of each auction and scored against what the month then paid, beside the full-history rank on the same targets, so the leakage is a number.

Every night

A run that fails loud, keeps its history and repairs its own feeds

At 03:30 a scheduled task runs all 117 stages in dependency order, pinned by a contract test. Each stage runs as its own child process while the parent holds an OS-level lock, so the night's peak memory is the largest single stage and two runs can never stack. Booking cannot lie: a return is OK, a deliberate exit is SKIP, and an exception, a child that dies without reporting or one that never exits is FAIL. Then the full suite runs (1,879 test cases) and the trading desk restarts on fresh artifacts. At 07:00 a watchdog checks the real-time price accumulator and, if the freshness manifest is more than 20 hours old, reruns the pipeline.

History is never lost

  • A vintage archive. Forecast and decision files are copied every night into a content-addressed, append-only archive (SHA-256). Every forward month's bid economics is frozen on every bid-window night, so any bid night can be rebuilt as it was.
  • Safe writes. History nothing else can rebuild is written atomically, keeps its previous version, and refuses any write that would shrink it.
  • Content, not file times. Critical inputs are checked by what they contain: the settlement lake's newest delivery date must be within three days, and a probe that fails reads as stale, never present. A 24-hour freshness guarantee is recorded per artifact.
  • Two backup drives, copied inside the lock, never mirror-deleted; restricted network data goes only to an allowlisted fixed disk, a rule pinned by a test.

Repair protocols

  • A failed feed is recorded, never hidden: marked down with its error, the run continues on the last good copy, and the desk says what the gap limits.
  • Transport ladders. NRC, then EIA, then archive; nodal prices from the API or ERCOT's archive branch, proven row-for-row identical first.
  • Retries that know the difference: timeouts, dropped connections, server errors and rate limits retry on a 15, 45 and 120-second backoff; a bad request or a missing page fails on the first attempt with its real error.
  • Holes diagnosed at the source. A guard probes every incomplete day of real-time prices at ERCOT, separates days ERCOT never posted from days it can fill, fills those, and escalates a stall after 72 hours.
  • Moved pages, refused connections. When two ERCOT listing pages moved and a state regulator's filing site refused the runtime's secure connection (October 2026), the stage recorded each feed down and finished; the repair probed the live pages, restored the connection with verification kept on, and shipped with tests.
  • Artifacts age in the open: a reconciliation that cannot reconcile a month refuses to restamp itself, and its freshness pill turns red.

The run diagnoses itself in its own words: "OURS to fix, not external"; "likely upstream holes, not a dead process"; "its output was LOST, not empty".

Built with AI agents, governed like a market desk

Seven contracts, and a rule for who decides

The build began on 2 July 2026 with Claude Code coding agents. Commits per day: 303 in July 2026, when the engine was built, then 22 in October. In between it ran every night on its own.

The founding specification binds seven contracts. Several exist because an earlier agent silently broke what they protect:

  1. Constraint-space doctrine. Model nodes and constraints, never source-sink pairs.
  2. Option valuation. Options pay the hourly positive part; option risk is the premium paid. (An earlier agent valued options off monthly means after being told otherwise.)
  3. Clearing-price regime. No structural auction discount assumed; every anchor carries an era tag.
  4. Forecasting-model boundary. A challenger never defines the baseline, never moves clearing cost, never overwrites a value.
  5. Awardable-MW non-filter. Informational columns never filter, rank or drop a candidate.
  6. Weather scenarios. Weather enters only as a scenario mixture with duration and probability.
  7. Residuals. One-offs become scenarios, never weights: "Uri is a state with a probability, not a February tilt".

Evidence promotes, humans check. Statistical gates (walk-forward skill, false-discovery control, oracle reconciliation, contract tests) promote automatically. Humans hold veto, one-click revert and sole execution authority. Human conjectures enter only signed, labeled and bannered in red. Every agent session reads a dated decision log before changing behavior, and a completeness matrix allows no silent "done".

Bugs caught by validating, now permanent guards

  • The leading minus sign of the congestion identity was dropped, inverting every path. A contingency was multi-counted. The first replay against realized settlement caught both.
  • Month names sort alphabetically: max(auction_name) pinned the whole engine to May. Artifacts are now chosen by date.
  • ERCOT's archive prices carry a leading space. An untrimmed cast made them null, and greatest(0, NULL) = 0 silently zeroed option payouts. Ingest now strips, and refuses partitions over 1% null.
  • A missing day inside a partition reported "32/32 COMPLETE" for four months. Completeness is now checked by day, nightly.
  • A stale 0.87 sat on the desk while the artifact said 0.75. Desk numbers are now read live, never copied.
